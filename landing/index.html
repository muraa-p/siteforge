How it works

Three steps, no learning curve

Every control is labelled in plain English. If you can answer a few questions about your business, you can publish a site today.

Start from a template

16 sector templates, researched from real template galleries (HTML5 UP, WordPress and Wix patterns) rather than recolouring one layout 16 times. Each one has genuinely different pages and structure.

Make it yours

Colours, photos, wording, page order. Add or remove any of 12 page types — pricing, FAQ, testimonials, news, team, booking — and point any card at any page or URL.

Download and publish

You get a ZIP of static HTML, CSS and one small script. Drag it onto Vercel, Netlify, GitHub Pages or any free host. No build step, no database, no maintenance.

What actually works

The boring parts are finished, not "coming soon"

Most builders sell you a shell. These are the bits that usually don't work — they work here, verified in a real browser every build.

🛒

A cart that works

Add items, change quantities, live total, then check out over WhatsApp or email with the itemised order. No payments, no server, no fake cart.

🃏

Clickable cards

Menu, service, project and team cards open a detail dialog with prices, descriptions and a call to action. Esc closes it; inner links still work.

🧭

Navigation that never dead-ends

Every layout keeps its navigation on every page, and the sidebar gets a real hamburger on phones instead of a squashed link bar.

Yours afterwards

The export is readable, commented HTML with your text. Tweak it in any editor, or come back and change it in the builder.

No tracking, anywhere

No accounts, no cookies, no analytics, no fonts loaded from Google. Your draft stays in your own browser and is never sent to a server.

Straight answers

What it doesn't do (yet)

Better you know now than after you've built a site around it.

  • No payments or accounts. There is no login, no user database and no card processing. The cart hands your order off to WhatsApp or email, and the contact form opens the visitor's email app.
  • No content management system. Editing after export means editing the HTML (or coming back to the builder). There is no admin panel to hand to a client.
  • No cloud sync. Your draft lives in this browser's local storage. Download a copy to keep it, and don't clear your browser data.
  • Photos live inside the file. Images are compressed and embedded, so keep them small (roughly under 1500px) or your ZIP gets heavy.
  • One device at a time. Two open tabs can overwrite each other's draft — there is no sync yet. This is the first thing on the roadmap.
  • Early project. It works and is tested, but it is young. Expect rough edges and tell me about them.
